What is açaí sorbet, and how is it different from an açaí bowl base?

Açaí sorbet is a ready-to-scoop frozen dessert made from açaí, water and sugar. How it differs from a blended açaí bowl base, and exactly what goes into ours.

Açaí bowls are everywhere. Açaí sorbet is less common, and the two are often confused. They are close, but they are not the same thing. This post explains what sorbet is, what a bowl base usually is, and why a ready-to-scoop sorbet needs no blender.

What is sorbet?

Sorbet is a frozen dessert made from fruit, water and sugar. It is churned as it freezes, which is what makes it smooth enough to scoop. Ice cream starts with cream or milk. Sorbet starts with fruit. That is the difference in one sentence.

Our açaí sorbet follows that definition. Organic Açaí Original Sensation is made from organic açaí (60%), filtered water, organic cane sugar, organic agave syrup, a stabilizer (sodium alginate), natural flavor and an acidity regulator (citric acid). Fruit comes first on the label. All ten of our flavors are certified vegan (Vegan Australia).

What is açaí?

Açaí is the fruit of a palm that grows in the Amazon. The berries are small and dark purple, and they grow in clusters near the top of the palm. In northern Brazil, açaí is an everyday food, usually eaten as a thick purée. Our sorbet is made in the Brazilian Amazon, where açaí grows.

The color is the fruit's. Açaí sorbet is a deep purple, close to black in the tub.

What is an açaí bowl base?

An açaí bowl base is a blended, frozen açaí purée served in a bowl. In a café it is usually made to order. Frozen açaí purée goes into a blender with a little liquid, and often a banana or other fruit. The blender runs until the mix is thick. The base is poured into a bowl, and the toppings go on.

That method works, but it has moving parts. It needs a blender, a recipe for the mix, and a person to run it for every bowl. The texture depends on how much liquid goes in and how long the blender runs. Two bowls from the same counter can turn out differently.

How is açaí sorbet different from an açaí bowl base?

The difference is when the work happens. A bowl base is blended at the moment you order it. A sorbet is churned and frozen at the factory. By the time it reaches you, the work is done.

Three things follow from that.

  • No blender. Açaí sorbet is scooped straight from the pint, the cup or the bucket.
  • The same texture every time. The sorbet is made in one place, to one recipe, and pasteurized before it is frozen.
  • Ready to eat. Our packs say it plainly: ready to eat, no preparation required, serve frozen.

Sweetness is also fixed. Many bowl bases are sweetened at the blender with juice, banana or syrup, and the amount changes from bowl to bowl. In a sorbet the sugar is part of the recipe and is printed on the label.

Does sorbet still make an açaí bowl? Yes. Scoop it into a bowl and add toppings. We call it the açaí bowl, ready to scoop.

How much açaí is in açaí sorbet?

That depends on who makes it, so read the ingredient list. Fruit percentages are printed on every Austropical label. Organic Açaí Original Sensation is 60% açaí. Açaí Zero Sugar (Not a low calorie food.) is also 60% açaí. Açaí Banana Sunrise and Açaí Strawberry Crush are 40% açaí, with 6% banana or 6% strawberry.

Original Sensation is USDA certified organic. So are two of our fruit sorbets, Organic Mango and Organic Coconut. The other seven flavors in the range are not certified organic.

What formats does açaí sorbet come in?

Ours comes in three.

  • A pint, 500 mL (16.9 fl oz), in a paper tub with a foil seal and a paper lid. A pint holds about three 2/3-cup servings.
  • A Grab'n Go cup, 250 mL, with a wooden spoon included. One cup is one serving.
  • A 10.2 kg foodservice bucket, a white square pail with a snap lid, for cafés, campus dining and grab-and-go counters. One bucket holds about 69 servings of 2/3 cup (147 g).

All three carry the same recipe. Keep them frozen at 0 °F (−18 °C) or below.

Is açaí sorbet the same as frozen açaí packs?

No. Frozen açaí packs are purée sold in flat pouches for blending. They are an ingredient. Sorbet is a finished dessert: churned, sweetened, frozen and ready for a spoon. If you want to blend, the packs are made for that. If you want to scoop, sorbet is the shorter route.
